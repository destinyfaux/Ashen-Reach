## Implemented foundation

- Invite-based join, local character creation/save, reconnect by session ID, player/DM roles, authoritative WebSocket state patches and intents.
- Client-predicted exploration movement with server validation; turn ownership and movement budgets apply only during active combat, while out-of-combat movement remains free. Combat Dash adds one effective speed to the turn budget; the client movement gate and HUD use the authoritative expanded budget. Rejected positions are reconciled, diagonal movement resolves walls axis-by-axis, and remote actors interpolate timestamped server positions. Accepted movement does not echo a full token snapshot to its predicting connection; the user confirmed the self-movement stutter is fixed in a two-window playtest. RTT ping is shown in the location HUD.
- Monster tokens/map spawn details are filtered for players by server-side range/line of sight; DM retains full visibility. Encounters begin when living monsters see players within 45 ft or hear out-of-combat attacks (60 ft) and spells (75 ft), with each intersected normal wall attenuating sound by 30 ft and each closed door by 35 ft. Alert calls travel up to two 40 ft hops; hidden monsters and unrelated/distant creatures are excluded. Awareness state stays server-private.
- 5E rules foundation: character sheets, dice, initiative/combat actions, monster AI, death saves, short/long rests, XP/rewards and inventory actions. See `server/src/rules/` and tests for exact coverage.
- Downed player characters are immobile and use a persistent downed pose rather than the death dissolve. An adjacent living ally can use Help to revive them at 1 HP; when no living online ally is available, the downed player can wait or confirm the end of the adventure. Dead saved characters are rejected on re-entry for the lifetime of the in-memory server session. Space triggers a local collision-safe jump; footstep cues follow the local gait's footfall phase.
- Doors have server-authoritative open/closed/locked/secret state. Players can toggle nearby unlocked doors with E; the DM can lock/unlock, open/close and reveal/conceal doors. Closed leaves block movement and sight on both server and client, opening synchronizes an animated leaf and procedural sound, and unrevealed secret doors are sent to players as solid walls without secret metadata.
- The live app starts at 10:00 in the deterministic 112×96 Ashen Reach home region (seed `73021`): a town-center spawn, nine landscape/settlement biomes, uneven terrain, connected roads and rivers/lakes, clustered props, four town-service building facades, ruins/caves, and a cemetery crypt entrance. Broadened mountain profiles and blended river valleys keep adjacent terrain vertices within 5 ft across 22 tested seeds; water is capped against local terrain and shoreline surfaces sit below it. The crypt exit returns to the overworld. Generated worlds have no enemy placements yet; town buildings are scenery, not working shops or services.
- P3.3 streamed world (PCG v2): the overworld is an infinite grid of 112×96-cell regions keyed `(rx, rz)` (`server/src/maps/world.mjs`). The home region (0,0) is the untouched `makeWorld` output with its boundary ring stitched open; neighbor regions generate from pure world-space fields: continuous macro biome weights (mountain/blight/snow/forest) drive both elevation (rolling hills, ridged mountain massifs with plateau terracing, blighted flats with spires) and per-cell biome classification, so biomes span regions organically — a meadow halo surrounds home through ring ~2 (fading out by ring ~4.6), forest skirts mountains, blight creeps out of forests. A deterministic window-invariant Jacobi slope clamp (≤4.2 ft/vertex) keeps terrain grades within budget; seams stay continuous by construction (≤5 ft asserted across rings, a 5×5 band, and mountain-country pairs). Water: home's river extends ~2 regions south into a terminus lake; further rivers valley-seek 3-6 regions from directional highland sources into surveyed large lakes (13-22 cells across) under hard separation rules (26-cell course spacing, 130-cell source spacing, 8-cell hard buffer, 12-cell open-land gate) that prevent parallel twin channels; ponds are biome-locked to lush regions; roads route around water with per-cluster bridges. Deterministic sites: exactly one ring-1 region carries a walled graveyard (gate, fences, marker/mound rows, mausoleum, columbaria) adjacent to the town; lush meadow/forest regions roll ruined temples or fortress keeps; mountains get cave mouths — all rendered by dedicated client prop builders. The server registers the focus region plus a 1-ring (pruning beyond a 2-ring), validates movement against the loaded region set in world feet, and shifts the party focus seamlessly when a token's cell crosses a border (no repositioning; deferred during combat). Clients fetch region defs from `/api/map/:id` into a cache, build them additively at their world offsets with fog of war scoped to dungeons (the overworld renders fully revealed), and prune beyond the ring; during a region build `terrainHeightAt` samples the region's own terrain, fixing the floating grass/prop bug in streamed windows. A top-right HUD minimap (`engine/worldProbe.ts` + `ui/hud.ts`) shows explored biome cells, water/roads, region borders, N/E/S/W compass letters, the party arrow, and a `Region (rx, rz) · cell` readout. Neighbor regions carry deterministic names (e.g. `w1_0` = "The Briar Fells") and no enemy spawns.
- Map transitions are server-validated, movement is bounded to the active map, water blocks movement on client and server, and per-map creature/door state plus party positions are retained in memory for the current session. Character inventory remains with each party token. The old threshold map remains registered for compatibility but is no longer the intended route.
- The registered starter dungeon is a deterministic, generated crypt (`seed=41003`, medium) named `ashen_cellar`. Generator modules include an entry, ossuary, gallery, chapel, guard chamber, sanctum, and boss arena, with socket metadata and tests. `ashen_cellar.json` remains an authored-map source/reference.
- Three.js world renderer uses procedural Canvas textures, `MeshStandardMaterial`, shadows, fog, procedural geometry/props, area tileset profiles, module floor inlays and wall supports. Atmospheric systems include dynamic sun/moon volumetric light shafts with Mie scattering and animated beam noise, an analytical exponential height fog volume with solar in-scattering, tiered undulating rolling ground mist with radial feathering, and dynamic terrain-conforming cloud shadows drifting across the landscape. The sky dome features a continuous 24-hour non-linear atmospheric model with rich golden hour transitions (orange, pink, peach, magenta), a high-resolution procedural moon with basalt maria and impact craters, glowing solar corona, and a planar troposphere FBM cloud canopy with modeled self-shadowing, silver linings, and nocturnal illumination (clouds shade to dark slate-charcoal with silver highlights at night rather than flat white). Sunlight dynamically dims when clouds pass overhead, and crepuscular god rays break through cloud gaps. Postprocessing uses `EffectComposer` with `RenderPass` → `GTAOPass` (depth-buffer-raymarched ambient occlusion) → `UnrealBloomPass` (0.30/0.42/0.85 — restrained halos on fire, emissives and magical VFX only) → `OutputPass` (ACES + sRGB at chain end) → a runtime-built 32³ LUT color grade with soft vignette (`engine/grade.ts`; muted Elden-Ring-style tone: desaturated mids, cool slate shadows, warm amber highlights). Outdoor grass uses 6-blade clumps with height variation (0.44 to 1.18 ft) and stratified boundary-crossing jitter for natural density and coverage; the water material uses shadow-receiving `MeshStandardMaterial` with `onBeforeCompile` wave normal perturbations, Fresnel sky/moon reflections, beach foam/ripples, cloud shadow dimming, and dark grounded albedo that naturally attenuates under nighttime lighting. Low-graphics mode cleanly disables heavyweight volumetrics and bloom.
- Opt-in Web Audio synthesizes 16 thematic musical pieces (menu, town, forest, tavern, graveyard, cathedral, castle, ruins, library, abyss, dungeon, crypt, combat, boss, victory, gameover) with cross-fading scene tracks and dynamic zone/region routing. Real-time zone resolution (`client/src/audio/zoneMusic.ts`) drives track playback by player coordinates, region biomes, building facades (inn/tavern), points of interest (cemetery, temples, keeps, ruins, caves), dungeon room modules (chapel, arena), boss encounters, victory fanfares, and defeat epitaphs. Procedural cues cover footsteps, nearby torch crackle, UI/type sounds, attacks, spells, healing, buffs, hits, misses, criticals and deaths. Browser UI behavior is checked; the listening/mix acceptance test remains open.
- The join screen has a low-contrast animated sigil behind its vignette, staggered title-letter glints, and an accessible five-click crown easter egg. Sigil/title motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
- HUD includes a compact bottom-left self-status plaque (portrait, name/archetype, AC, segmented vitality meter and capped condition chips), companion cards, initiative/turn indication, combat actions, DM tools, chat and RTT. Combat turns require choosing a visible active foe before Strike or Cantrip is enabled; action availability is shown and spent actions are disabled. Initiative order and movement state are filtered per player so hidden actors and their movement data are not sent to players; the DM retains the full encounter. Record/satchel/menu controls are a horizontal row above the plaque and collapse to accessible icon buttons at narrow widths. The self panel no longer uses `.ornate`, whose relative positioning had overridden its fixed bottom-left anchor and placed it over the upper-left location/time/weather plate. The upper-left location/party stack is height-bounded and scrollable. Self is omitted from companion cards; party data is deduplicated by ID; movement denials do not enter persistent chat. Screenshot review is still needed.
- Character Creation Visage & 3D Avatar System: Step 4 (Visage) and Step 6 (Oath) feature an interactive 3D WebGL Three.js character viewport (`Visage3DView`) showing the player's real in-game model with class equipment sets, full 360-degree orbit and zoom, auto-rotation, and procedural idle breathing via `Animator`, alongside switchable painted 2D portraits. Customization includes masculine/feminine body forms (affecting shoulder/hip widths and torso geometry), 3 frame builds (slim/normal/heavy), 10 hairstyles (5 masculine, 5 feminine) with SVG icons and category filtering, 12 skin tones, 12 hair colors, 8 glowing soul eye colors, 6 cloak swatches, and 6 facial markings (scars, runes, warpaint, freckles, skull paint). Racial features directly shape the 3D model (elf pointed ears, tiefling horns & tail, dwarf braided beard, orc tusks, dragonborn crest & snout, and race stature scaling: 0.75x for small races, 1.14x for large races). All visual parameters are mapped by server `mapLookToRig`, synchronized across multiplayer sessions, and reflected in `TokenLook`.

## Known gaps (do not describe these as complete)

- No travel-time or route-selection systems, interactive town NPCs/shops/rest services, additional dungeon destinations, enemy placements in the overworld, durable map persistence, or multistory traversal. The world minimap/compass/region readout exist (P3.7 partial). The generated home region, streamed neighbor regions, and map progress exist only in the current in-memory session; a server restart resets them (neighbor regions regenerate deterministically; session-only state such as door state re-applies per region).
- The streamed world is code-verified (determinism, seam continuity, water grounding, road walkability, focus shifts, region REST, biome halo, graveyard/ruins sites, river→lake termination, pond lock) but has not been visually reviewed in a browser: rendered seam appearance, fog behavior across borders, mountain silhouette quality, the new ruin/graveyard props, and the minimap rendering still need a human pass. Route selection, travel time, and additional wilderness/dungeon destinations are not implemented (see TASKS.md P3.7). Roads, rivers, town composition, primitive scenery and water appearance still need visual iteration. Region generation (≈25-60 ms per region measured for PCG v2) and region-def fetch (≈150–250 KB each) happen on border approach; no hitch profile has been measured in a real client.
- Door locks and secret discovery are DM-managed; player key, lockpicking, and search/ability-check mechanics are not implemented.
- No general navigation/pathfinding service; monster alertness currently uses fixed sight/hearing ranges and simple wall/door attenuation rather than stealth, perception checks, or richer sensory rules.
- Combat target selection is a visible-active-foe dropdown; area-spell targeting and selecting multiple targets remain limited.
- Materials remain mostly procedural textures and primitive geometry. No mature authored model/texture pipeline, biome prop catalog, or demonstrated PBR art quality.
- Audio is synthesized and opt-in; no recorded/owned sound library, spatial panning/attenuation, robust per-surface footsteps, or sound occlusion. Volume controls and mute are implemented; acoustic balance and restraint still need human playtesting.
- RTT is implemented; packet loss, jitter, authoritative reconciliation telemetry, and a network diagnostics panel are not.
- UI fixes have automated type/build/smoke coverage but still need real multiplayer screenshots and focused layout/accessibility review. There is no known always-available VLM service; if one is unavailable, record that and use human/playwright evidence without inventing scores.
- Account/login and durable server-side character/world saves are not implemented. Character convenience saves are browser-local; authoritative session state is process-memory state.
- Character death and character-ID rejection are session-memory only; there is no account identity or durable server persistence. Jumping has no gameplay/collision-height effect, by design.
